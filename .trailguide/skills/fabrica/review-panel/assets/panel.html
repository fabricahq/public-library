Severity: does it hold up the merge?

Every finding has one of two severities. The synthesis re-judges each reviewer's severity rather than copying it.

blocking
Fix it, or decide it, before this change merges.A loop that drops the last item, a secret written to a log, a broken acceptance criterion.
advisory
Worth knowing or improving, but fine to merge without.A missing test for an edge case, a misleading comment, a slow path that isn't hot yet.

Category: what kind of problem is it?

Severity says how urgent a finding is; category says what to do about it.

defect
The change breaks the spec, a decision you supplied, or behavior the code already agreed on, or plainly does something wrong. Fix it.
policy
The right answer depends on a choice nobody has made yet. The finding names the choice and the options, and the synthesis turns it into a question for you.
negligible-likelihood
A real gap that needs an unlikely mix of events to bite. Usually: accept it and write it down.

The panel's verdict

The synthesis gives the change one verdict, and it must agree with the findings and questions under it.

ready
No blocking findings and no blocking questions. Merge when you're happy with the advisories.
fix-first
At least one blocking finding that can be fixed without a new decision from you. Tick them and prepare a handoff.
needs-decision
At least one question only you can answer is holding up the merge. Answer it, marked blocks merge, first.

Each reviewer's verdict

Follows from that reviewer's own findings, before the synthesis checks them.

pass
No findings. A good result when it's true.
concerns
Only advisory findings.
blocking
At least one blocking finding.

What it checked

Each reviewer lists what it checked, such as each acceptance criterion or each risk it traced, under a reviewer's What it checked tab.

met
Checked, and the change does what it should.
partly
Partly there. A finding explains the gap.
not met
Checked, and the change falls short. A finding explains why.
unverified
Couldn't be checked, for example a test it couldn't run or a spec it didn't have. Worth checking yourself.

How findings get merged

Reviewers work independently, so two of them often report the same problem. The synthesis reads the code behind every finding, then:

Merges
Writes each underlying problem once, as S1, S2 and so on. Open a row and choose Merges N reviewer findings to see the originals. Several reviewers agreeing raises confidence.
Drops
Removes a finding that is wrong, handled elsewhere, or out of scope, and records why. Tick Show every reviewer finding to see drops and check them.
Accounts
Every reviewer finding ends up merged or dropped exactly once, so nothing a reviewer raised goes missing.

Session status

queued
Waiting for a free slot.
running
Reading the change; the card shows what it's doing now.
done
Reported findings that met the output contract.
failed
Crashed, timed out, or never met the contract. The synthesis notes what wasn't covered.

A note like ran on Claude means a Codex reviewer fell back to Claude because Codex was missing or failed.

Handing off fixes

Tick the findings you want fixed, then choose Prepare handoff. You get a prompt to edit and paste into the agent that will make the fixes. It asks the agent to confirm each problem first, fix only those, add tests, and re-run the reviewers that raised them.

Your ticks are remembered in this browser for this run.
